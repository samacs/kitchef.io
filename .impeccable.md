## Design Context

### Users
Mexican home-food operators (cocineras) running small kitchens from home — selling tamales, pasteles, meal-prep, comida corrida. They manage everything from their phone on 4G. Their customers are local neighbors, office workers, and WhatsApp contacts ordering for delivery or pickup.

The operator's job: take orders, cook, deliver, get paid, repeat. Every screen should respect that she's running a real business from her kitchen.

The customer's job: find something delicious, order it, know when it arrives, pay without friction.

### Brand Personality
**Calm. Confident. Artisanal.**

Quiet confidence — no flash, no startup energy, no "AI-powered" anything. The design speaks through restraint: bone backgrounds, deep green accents held back, serif headlines that feel like a restaurant menu rather than a SaaS dashboard. The cocinera is the hero, not the platform.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone:** Warm-technical. Bone and green. Instrument Serif headlines give editorial weight; Inter UI text stays out of the way; JetBrains Mono prices feel precise without being cold.
- **References:** High-end restaurant menus, artisan bakery packaging, Mexican market signage (hand-painted lettering). The confidence of Stripe's docs + the warmth of a neighborhood taquería.
- **Anti-references:** Flowbite/Bootstrap generic. Neon gradients. Emoji-as-UI. Purple SaaS palettes. "Growth hacker" aesthetics. Uber Eats commodity feel.
- **Theme:** Light mode default (bone `#F8F6F1`), dark mode supported. Never pure white page backgrounds; never pure black.

### Checkout Emotional Goal
**Delight + anticipation.** "I can already smell the tamales." The checkout should make the customer excited about the food — the tip feels generous not obligatory, the payment method reveal feels informative not bureaucratic. Every detail says "this is a real kitchen run by a real person."

### Design Principles
1. **Respect the cocinera.** She's a CEO running a business from her kitchen. Never condescend, never over-simplify, never make the UI look like a toy.
2. **One accent, held back.** Deep green `#0A5A3C` is the only accent. Use it for CTAs, active states, and positive deltas. Everything else is ink, bone, and line.
3. **Typography carries the hierarchy.** Instrument Serif for importance, Inter for function, JetBrains Mono for money. The fonts do the work so color doesn't have to.
4. **Mexican-native, not translated.** tú forms, pesos, colonias, platillos. The UI should feel like it was born in Mexico City, not localized from English.
5. **Quiet confidence over visual noise.** No gradients, no emoji-as-UI, no bouncy animations. 150ms transitions. The design earns trust by being predictable and precise.
